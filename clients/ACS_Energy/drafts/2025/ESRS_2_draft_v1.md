The scope of consolidation includes all subsidiaries over which the Company exercises control, as defined in the applicable financial reporting framework (EU-adopted IFRS). Where data from the value chain could not be obtained directly from suppliers or customers, the Company employs estimates based on industry-average emission factors, statistical models and extrapolation from primary data collected from a representative sample of value chain partners. The underlying methodology, including data sources and assumptions, is described in ESRS E1 and further elaborated in the value chain estimation note in BP-2. ## GOV-5 — Risk management and internal controls over sustainability reporting

ACS Energy applies a risk management framework for sustainability reporting that mirrors the financial reporting internal control system. The framework consists of three lines of defence: operational data owners (generation plants, commercial units), a central Sustainability Reporting Function under the CSO, and an Internal Audit team that conducts periodic reviews of data quality and control effectiveness.

Key risks identified include estimation uncertainty in scope 3 emissions, data aggregation errors from multiple ERP systems across three countries, and reliance on third-party providers for customer consumption data. ## IRO-1 — Description of process to identify and assess material IROs

The identification and assessment of material impacts and risks is conducted through a structured double materiality process overseen by the Sustainability Department, with cross-functional input. The process consists of four steps: (1) **Impact mapping** – using life-cycle analysis and value chain mapping to identify actual and potential impacts on the environment and people; (2) **Risk and opportunity identification** – scanning regulatory (EU ETS, CBAM), market (energy prices), reputational and physical climate risks; (3) **Stakeholder input** – surveys and workshops with investors, NGOs, employees and community representatives; (4) **Assessment and prioritisation** – scoring impacts on severity (scale, scope, irremediability) and likelihood, and risks/opportunities on financial magnitude and probability.

For opportunities specifically, the process incorporates a forward-looking technology and market assessment (e.g. hydrogen potential, battery storage) and a governance factor evaluation (e.g. policy support for renewables). All material IROs are validated by the Management Board and reviewed by the Supervisory Board. The assessment is updated annually or when a significant change occurs (e.g. new regulation, major investment). The methodology is documented and aligned with EFRAG’s implementation guidance.
