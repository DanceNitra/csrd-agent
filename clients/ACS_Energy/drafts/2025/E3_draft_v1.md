## E3-1 — Policies related to water and marine resources

These policies explicitly address water consumption, water withdrawals, wastewater discharges, and the protection of marine resources, recognizing that thermal power generation relies heavily on cooling water (78% of total withdrawals). The policies are aligned with the European Union’s Water Framework Directive (2000/60/EC) and the Marine Strategy Framework Directive (2008/56/EC), and they incorporate the principle of sustainable water use at basin level. Geographic scope covers all operated sites, with enhanced measures for the Czech Republic, where water stress has been identified as significant.

The policies consider water-stressed areas by requiring site-specific risk assessments. In the Czech Republic, where 5% of total water consumption and 8% of withdrawals occur under stress conditions, additional mitigation measures are triggered, including cooling tower optimisation and alternative water sourcing studies. The policies further mandate compliance with local abstraction permits and discharge consents, and they encourage third-party audits to ensure alignment with emerging EU regulatory requirements.

## E3-2 — Actions and resources related to water and marine resources

To reduce water consumption and improve efficiency, ACS Energy implements a multi-year action plan covering all power generation assets. Key actions include the installation of closed-loop cooling systems at two of three thermal plants, scheduled for completion by 2026, and the retrofit of dry cooling technology at one gas-fired peaker plant. At the 12 wind turbines and 2 solar farms, water use is minimal, but rainwater harvesting is being trialled for panel cleaning at the solar sites.

Investment in water treatment and recycling infrastructure totalled €4.2 million in the reporting period. This included a new membrane bioreactor at the largest thermal plant, enabling the reuse of 5.2 million m³ of process water (equivalent to 12% of total withdrawals). An additional €1.8 million has been allocated in the 2025 budget for advanced reverse osmosis units at two Czech facilities. Total CAPEX for water management was €6.1 million (0.5% of revenue), and OPEX reached €3.9 million, covering operation of treatment plants and monitoring equipment.

Partnerships supporting water stewardship include membership in the European Water Stewardship program and collaboration with local utilities in the Czech Republic to share best practices on water reuse. Marine protection initiatives are limited given the inland location of all assets; however, a zero-discharge protocol for cooling water biocides is applied at all thermal plants to prevent harm to downstream aquatic ecosystems. No direct marine resource extraction occurs.
