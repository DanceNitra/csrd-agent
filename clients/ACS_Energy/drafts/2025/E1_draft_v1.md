## E1-1 — Transition plan for climate change mitigation

ACS Energy has adopted a formal transition plan for climate change mitigation, which is aligned with the Paris Agreement’s 1.5°C pathway and the EU ‘Fit for 55’ legislation. The plan has been approved by the Board of Directors and is embedded in our corporate strategy. The plan sets GHG emission reduction targets of 55% by 2030, 75% by 2040, and 90% by 2050 compared to a 2019 base year (base-year emissions: 4,100,000 tCO₂e across Scope 1, 2, and 3).  

Key decarbonisation levers identified in the transition plan include: (i) accelerating the phase-out of coal-fired power generation; (ii) expanding renewable energy capacity (wind and solar) from the current 18% of generation mix to 35% by 2028 and 65% by 2035; (iii) investing in energy efficiency improvements across our thermal plant fleet and grid infrastructure; (iv) deploying carbon capture and storage (CCS) feasibility studies for residual emissions; and (v) promoting electrification of customer end-uses via smart grid solutions.  

The transition plan is supported by an indicative investment and funding framework. Initial estimates allocate €450 million in CapEx over 2024–2030 for renewable build-out and grid modernisation, with additional €150 million for plant retrofits and CCS pilots. Full funding details, including the use of green bonds and EU Just Transition Fund instruments, are currently under development. The plan is consistent with the EU Taxonomy’s technical screening criteria for climate change mitigation, though a full taxonomy alignment assessment for all activities is planned for completion by 2025. ## E1-2 — Policies related to climate change mitigation and adaptation

ACS Energy has established a comprehensive Climate Change Policy covering both mitigation and adaptation, approved by the Executive Board in 2022. The mitigation policy commits the company to reducing GHG emissions across all scopes in line with our science-based targets, to increasing the share of renewable energy in our generation portfolio, and to phasing out unabated coal-fired generation by 2035. The policy applies to all own operations (power plants, grids, offices) and extends to key value chain activities, including procurement of fuel and equipment, and customer engagement on energy efficiency.  

Our Climate Adaptation Policy addresses physical climate risks such as increasing frequency of heatwaves, droughts, and extreme precipitation events affecting our generation and transmission assets. The policy mandates climate risk assessments for all major infrastructure projects (e.g., new wind farms, grid upgrades) and requires the integration of climate resilience measures into asset design and maintenance schedules. The adaptation policy covers own operations and, where practicable, influences suppliers of critical infrastructure components. Both policies are reviewed annually and are publicly disclosed on our website, in line with ESRS E1 requirements and the recommendations of the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD).

## E1-3 — Actions and resources related to climate change

To implement our climate policies, ACS Energy has launched a portfolio of mitigation and adaptation actions. For mitigation, key initiatives include the construction of two new wind farms (total capacity 80 MW) by 2025, the repowering of existing wind turbines, and the installation of solar panels on distribution substations. ## E1-4 — Targets related to climate change mitigation and adaptation

ACS Energy has set the following GHG emission reduction targets using 2019 as the base year: 55% absolute reduction in total GHG emissions (Scope 1, 2, and 3) by 2030; 75% reduction by 2040; and 90% reduction by 2050. These targets have been submitted to the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) for validation and are consistent with the 1.5°C scenario. As of the end of 2023, total GHG emissions (Scope 1+2+3) were 3,377,000 tCO₂e (preliminary), representing a 17.6% reduction from the base year of 4,100,000 tCO₂e. Progress has been driven primarily by increased wind generation and reduced coal dispatch.  

In addition to absolute targets, we track GHG intensity per net revenue. The 2023 intensity was 2.81 tCO₂e per €1,000 revenue (3,377,000 tCO₂e / €1,200,000,000). Our 2030 intensity target is 1.41 tCO₂e per €1,000 revenue, consistent with the absolute reduction pathway. Adaptation targets include ensuring that 100% of major generation assets (thermal and renewable) have completed a climate vulnerability assessment by 2026, and that all new grid investments incorporate climate resilience standards by 2027.

## E1-8 — Internal carbon pricing

ACS Energy applies an internal carbon price as a tool to guide investment and operational decisions. A shadow price of €85 per tonne of CO₂e (Scope 1) is used in the economic evaluation of all new capital projects with a value above €5 million. This price is reviewed annually and is intended to reflect the projected EU ETS price trajectory and the social cost of carbon.  

Additionally, the company operates an internal carbon fund: business units that exceed their emissions budget must pay a fee of €85 per tonne of excess emissions into the fund, which is then reinvested in emission reduction projects across the group. This mechanism has been operational since 2022 and has generated approximately €3.2 million in 2023. The internal carbon price covers Scope 1 emissions from own generation assets; Scope 2 and Scope 3 emissions are not yet formally priced, though this is under consideration for future expansion.

## E1-9 — Anticipated financial effects from material physical and transition risks

Climate-related physical risks, particularly increased frequency of drought and heatwaves, are expected to affect ACS Energy’s thermal power generation (reduced cooling water availability) and transmission line capacity (lower efficiency). Based on preliminary scenario analysis, annual financial impacts from physical risks are estimated at €12–18 million (0.8–1.5% of EBITDA) by 2030, mainly due to reduced generation output and higher maintenance costs. Adaptation investments (e.g., upgrades to circulation systems, grid hardening) are expected to mitigate approximately 40% of these impacts.  

Transition risks, including carbon pricing under the EU ETS and regulatory tightening, constitute a more significant financial exposure. For our coal-fired assets, the cost of carbon allowances at current EU ETS prices (~€80/tCO₂) could add €196 million annually to operating costs if no abatement actions are taken. The transition plan’s accelerated coal phase-out is designed to reduce this exposure. We also identify opportunities: growth in renewable energy generation is projected to generate incremental revenue of €150–200 million by 2035 from new wind and solar capacity, supported by power purchase agreements and green tariff premiums. Detailed financial quantification of these effects will be included in our next annual report as methodologies mature.
