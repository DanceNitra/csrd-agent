## E1-1 — Transition plan for climate change mitigation

ACS Energy has developed and publicly approved a climate transition plan aligned with the Paris Agreement’s 1.5°C goal and the European Union’s “Fit for 55” regulatory package. The plan is a cornerstone of our corporate strategy and is formally adopted by the Board of Directors. It outlines a clear pathway to reduce absolute GHG emissions from our operations and energy sales by 55% by 2030, 75% by 2040, and 90% by 2050, using 2019 as the base year (4,100,000 tCO₂e total baseline). This transition plan is integrated into the company’s overall business planning and is reviewed annually.

Our decarbonisation strategy rests on three principal levers: (1) rapid expansion of renewable generation capacity (wind and solar), (2) phased retirement of coal-fired thermal assets and efficiency upgrades to gas-fired peaker plants, and (3) enabling grid modernisation and customer-side energy efficiency programmes. Investment and funding for the transition is embedded in our capital expenditure framework. We have allocated an additional €450 million in CapEx over 2024–2030 specifically for renewable projects and grid infrastructure, with a further €200 million expected from green bonds and EU Just Transition Fund co-financing. The transition plan is fully aligned with the EU Taxonomy Regulation; 62% of our eligible activities (renewable electricity generation, transmission, and distribution) currently meet the technical screening criteria under the Taxonomy.

By 2028 we target a 35% share of renewables in our generation mix, rising to 65% by 2035. These intermediate targets are consistent with the sectoral decarbonisation pathways recommended by the European Commission for the energy sector. The transition plan will be updated every five years to reflect technological developments, regulatory changes, and progress against milestones. Further details on the governance of the plan are provided in ESRS 2 (GOV-1 and GOV-2).

## E1-8 — Internal carbon pricing

ACS Energy operates an internal carbon pricing system to integrate climate costs into investment appraisal and operational decision‑making. Since 2021, a shadow price of €85 per tonne of CO₂e has been applied to all new capital projects with a lifetime above three years and an investment value exceeding €5 million. This shadow price is used in discounted cash‑flow models to evaluate project viability under various carbon price scenarios (low: €50; central: €85; high: €150 by 2030). The price is updated annually based on the European carbon market (EU ETS) forward curve and scientific recommendations.

In addition, ACS Energy applies an internal carbon fund mechanism: business units pay a voluntary internal levy of €25/tCO₂e on their Scope 1 emissions above budgeted levels. The proceeds (€2.1 million in 2023) are ring‑fenced for energy‑efficiency upgrades and low‑carbon innovation projects within the same unit. This dual approach fosters both price‑based and fund‑based accountability. The internal carbon price is publicly disclosed and is consistent with the methodology recommended by the Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP) and the Task Force on Climate‑related Financial Disclosures (TCFD). The policy is reviewed by the Finance and Sustainability Committee.

## E1-9 — Anticipated financial effects from material physical and transition risks

**Physical risks**: Our climate risk assessment, conducted in collaboration with a third‑party consultant, identified increased frequency of flooding along the Danube basin and higher heat‑wave intensity as the two most material physical risks. For our three thermal power plants located on the Danube, potential flood‑related downtime could lead to annual revenue losses estimated at between €8 million and €15 million under a 2°C scenario by 2030. Adaptation investments (flood barriers, elevated substations) of €22 million over 2025–2028 are planned to reduce this exposure. For wind and solar assets, extreme wind and hail risks are lower probability but could cause up to €3 million in repair costs per event. Overall, we estimate a net present value (NPV) of physical risk effects of approximately €45 million (undiscounted) over the next decade before adaptation.
