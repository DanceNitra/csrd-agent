## S3-1 — Policies related to affected communities

Enel has established a comprehensive policy framework to govern its relationships with affected communities, anchored in our Human Rights Policy (aligned with the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights) and our Sustainability Policy. These policies explicitly recognise the rights of local communities, including indigenous peoples, and set out commitments to free, prior and informed consent (FPIC), benefit‑sharing, and ongoing dialogue. Regarding indigenous peoples, Enel’s *Indigenous Peoples Policy* (updated in 2022) is fully aligned with the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP) and the ILO Convention 169. The policy mandates FPIC processes for any project that may affect indigenous lands, territories, or resources. Human rights due diligence covering affected communities is integrated into our Group‑wide Human Rights Due Diligence (HRDD) framework, which includes site‑level risk assessments, third‑party audits, and regular monitoring. These policies are publicly available on our website and are embedded in contractual agreements with joint‑venture partners and key suppliers.

## S3-2 — Processes for engaging with affected communities

Enel maintains structured engagement processes that ensure regular, culturally appropriate consultation with affected communities. At each operational site, a dedicated community liaison officer (or team) is responsible for organising public meetings, surveys, and information sessions. In Italy, Spain, and Romania, local community forums meet at least quarterly during the construction phase of major projects and annually during operations. Our approach prioritises early engagement – before final investment decisions – and follows the principles of free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) where indigenous or traditional communities are involved.

For indigenous peoples specifically, Enel has established FPIC protocols that are integrated into our project development lifecycle. These protocols require independent facilitation, village‑level consultations, and documented consent before any land‑use or construction activity begins. Grievance mechanisms – both formal (via our Ethics Line and Ombudsperson) and informal (through community liaison structures) – are available to all community members, ensuring accessibility regardless of language or literacy level. In 2024, we operated 47 community liaison offices across the three countries, with an average response time to community queries of 10 working days.

## S3-3 — Remediation of negative impacts

Enel is committed to remediating any adverse impacts on affected communities caused by our operations. Our *Remediation Framework* for community impacts categorises grievances by severity and assigns dedicated teams to investigate and resolve each case. Where harm is identified, we implement corrective action plans that include compensation, restoration, or other remedies agreed with the affected parties. In line with the UN Guiding Principles, we also provide access to operational‑level grievance mechanisms that do not preclude recourse to judicial or State‑based processes.

In 2024, Enel received 23 formal grievances from community members related to land‑use, noise, dust, and local employment issues. Of these, 18 were resolved by year‑end, with an average resolution time of 45 days. The remaining five are under active mediation. Additionally, Enel runs community benefit‑sharing programs, such as local employment initiatives, infrastructure contributions, and renewable energy access projects. In Italy, our *Communities of Tomorrow* program invested €2.8 million in 2024 in social‑enterprise partnerships near wind and solar parks. Land rights issues are addressed through dedicated land acquisition standards that ensure fair compensation and voluntary consent; no outstanding land‑rights conflicts were reported in 2024.

## S3-4 — Targets related to affected communities

Enel has set several quantitative targets to drive accountability and continuous improvement in community relations. Our *Sustainable Communities 2026* goals include:
- **Community investment target:** spend at least 0.5% of annual pre‑tax profit on community investment and development programs (baseline year 2022).
- **Grievance resolution time:** resolve 90% of community grievances within 60 days.
- **Engagement frequency:** conduct at least one dedicated community engagement session per year at each operation site with high‑risk or vulnerable community proximity.
- **Benefit‑sharing:** ensure that 100% of new renewable projects above 50 MW have a formal benefit‑sharing agreement in place before construction start.

The base year for all community‑related targets is 2022, with progress reported annually in our Sustainability Report. In 2024, community investment reached €4.2 million (against the 0.5% profit target, which equates to approximately €6 million – gap explained by project timelines). Grievance resolution time averaged 45 days, already exceeding the 60‑day target. Engagement sessions were completed at 89% of applicable sites; two sites missed the target due to permitting delays.

## S3-5 — Metrics for affected communities

Enel monitors a range of metrics to assess its impacts on and relationships with affected communities. In 2024, we identified 14 operations sites located within 1 km of marginalised or vulnerable communities (based on national socio‑economic indicators). Community engagement sessions totalled 112 across all countries, with an average of 8 participants per session. Community‑related incidents (protests, conflicts) numbered two minor protests in Romania related to grid extension works; both were resolved through dialogue within two weeks. Local employment rate in operations stands at 72% of workforce hired from within 50 km of sites (based on voluntary reporting).
